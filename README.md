# Aircraft Screws, Bolts & Nuts: Essential Threaded Hardware for Aviation Maintenance

Aircraft screws, bolts, and nuts are essential threaded fasteners used throughout airframe structures, mechanical assemblies, equipment installations, engine areas, access panels, and aircraft interiors. These components provide removable connections that allow assemblies to be securely joined while still permitting inspection, servicing, adjustment, and replacement during aircraft maintenance.

Although screws, bolts, and nuts may appear similar to conventional industrial hardware, aviation applications can require specific dimensions, materials, strength characteristics, thread configurations, head styles, locking arrangements, and finishes. The correct selection depends on the assembly in which the hardware is installed and the requirements specified by the applicable aircraft documentation.

## Aircraft Bolts for High-Strength Mechanical Connections

Aircraft bolts are commonly used where a strong removable connection is required between two or more components. They can be found in structural fittings, control mechanisms, landing gear assemblies, engine-related installations, equipment mounts, brackets, and numerous other mechanical applications. Different bolt configurations are designed for different installation environments and load requirements.

Bolt selection can involve considerations such as diameter, length, thread type, head configuration, material, strength, and surface treatment. Some aircraft bolts are designed for applications where shear loads are significant, while others are selected for tensile or combined loading. The complete fastening assembly can also include washers, nuts, locking devices, or other supporting hardware.

## Aircraft Screws for Panels and Equipment Installations

Aircraft screws are widely used in applications where components need to be removed repeatedly during inspection or maintenance. They can be found on access panels, fairings, interior trim, equipment covers, avionics installations, cockpit panels, and various aircraft fittings. Their removable nature makes them particularly useful for assemblies that require regular access.

Different screw designs can have different head configurations, thread forms, lengths, materials, and drive types. Countersunk screws can be used where a flush surface is required, while other head styles may be selected where clearance and installation requirements permit a protruding head. Captive screw arrangements can also help prevent hardware from becoming separated from frequently removed panels.

## Aircraft Nuts and Locking Hardware

Aircraft nuts are commonly used with bolts and threaded studs to create removable fastening assemblies. Depending on the application, nuts may incorporate different locking features or may be used together with separate locking devices. The selection of the nut must correspond with the thread characteristics and configuration of the mating fastener.

Locking is particularly important in aircraft environments because vibration and repeated loading can affect threaded joints. Different locking arrangements may be used according to the application, including self-locking nuts and other approved methods of securing threaded connections. The appropriate method is determined by the applicable aircraft maintenance or engineering requirements.

## Matching Screws, Bolts, and Nuts to Aircraft Applications

A threaded fastener assembly normally works as a combination of components rather than as an isolated part. A bolt may require a particular nut and washer arrangement, while a screw may need a compatible insert, nut plate, or retaining system. Differences in thread form, diameter, length, material, or locking characteristics can affect whether the complete assembly is appropriate.

Aircraft structures can also impose specific requirements on fastener installation. The surrounding material, joint configuration, accessibility, corrosion environment, clearance, and expected loading can all influence hardware selection. ## Materials and Finishes for Aircraft Threaded Hardware

Aircraft screws, bolts, and nuts can be manufactured from different materials according to the mechanical and environmental requirements of their intended application. Steel, stainless steel, titanium, aluminum alloys, and other materials may be encountered in aviation hardware. Material selection can influence strength, weight, corrosion resistance, temperature capability, and compatibility with adjacent components.

Surface finishes and protective treatments may also be specified for particular aircraft hardware. These treatments can help address corrosion or environmental exposure and may form part of the original hardware specification. Consequently, changing the material or finish simply because another component has similar dimensions may not provide an acceptable replacement.

Aircraft parts distributors can improve procurement accuracy by recording complete fastener specifications rather than only generic descriptions such as "aircraft bolt" or "aircraft screw." Part number, size, material, finish, thread characteristics, head configuration, locking method, and quantity can all contribute to a more precise sourcing requirement.
